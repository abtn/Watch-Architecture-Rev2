Two-reviewer protocol (RW-D30): every output is independently scored by two reviewers who receive anonymized outputs without condition labels. They submit scores before discussion; both original scores are retained. Material disagreements are adjudicated by a third reviewer or resolved using a documented process, with the resolution preserved rather than overwriting the initial assessments.

Reviewer-disagreement triggers (RW-D31): require formal resolution when total scores differ by more than 10/100, reviewers disagree on the presence of a critical factual error, or they disagree on whether a central claim is unsupported or materially misleading. Preserve both initial assessments and the resolution record.

Reviewer qualifications (RW-D32): reviewers need relevant science/technology literacy and evidence-evaluation ability, but need not be professional journalists. Claim verification requires the ability to inspect the fixed source packet and verify claims against it; editorial assessment requires understanding of scientific communication, uncertainty, and evidence-based reasoning.

Reviewer assignment (RW-D33): use the same two reviewers for all 20 outputs. Randomize anonymized presentation order and distribute work across balanced batches with breaks. Preserve order and batch metadata so fatigue and order effects can be examined.

Reviewer calibration (RW-D34): before scored evaluation, both reviewers independently score the same sample analyses excluded from the 20 experimental runs using the frozen rubric. They then discuss discrepancies and clarify ambiguous scoring anchors; repeat calibration if needed. The goal is consistent interpretation, not forced agreement on every judgment.

Calibration acceptance criteria (RW-D35): calibration is accepted when reviewers apply the critical-factual-error definition consistently, interpret both 50-point rubric components consistently, and have no unresolved ambiguity in scoring anchors that could materially affect results. No numerical inter-rater reliability threshold is imposed; the calibration sample is too small for such a threshold to provide meaningful assurance.

Claim-verification rubric structure (RW-D36): the 50-point claim-verification component is organized around factual accuracy against the source packet, evidence traceability, quotation and attribution accuracy, and numerical and temporal accuracy (including figures, dates, and units). Exact point allocations and detailed anchors remain open until the newsroom assignment and source-grounded answer key are prepared, avoiding arbitrary weighting before the actual claims and evidence are known.

Editorial-assessment rubric structure (RW-D37): the 50-point editorial component covers interpretation of findings, significance without exaggeration, uncertainty and limitations, and synthesis across sources into a coherent analysis. Exact point allocations and detailed anchors remain open until the newsroom assignment and source-grounded answer key are prepared.

Critical factual error definition (RW-D38): a critical error is a materially consequential false, unsupported, or misleading claim that changes the reader's understanding of the central finding, its strength, implications, or evidentiary basis. Examples include reversing the central finding; presenting correlation as demonstrated causation; inventing or materially altering a result, quotation, or statistic; omitting a limitation in a materially conclusion-changing way; or misattributing a finding. Minor wording issues and non-material inaccuracies are excluded. Apply the definition against the frozen source packet and answer key; examples are illustrative, not exhaustive.

Critical-error counting and reporting (RW-D39): count each distinct underlying critical factual error once per output, even if repeated; count genuinely different errors separately. For control and treatment separately, report both total critical errors and the number of outputs affected. Compare these counts under the predeclared critical-error acceptance gate.


Source-grounded answer key (RW-D40): prepare the key before scored runs and independently of model-generated outputs. For each expected factual claim, record its source and exact location, supported statement, qualifications/limitations, central-or-secondary importance, and known ambiguity or disagreement between sources. Identify important facts the packet does not establish, potentially unsupported or misleading claims, and acceptable alternative interpretations. The key is not a closed list of approved wording: reviewers may credit novel claims when their underlying factual content is accurate and supported by the frozen packet. Freeze the answer key with the corpus and scored task brief before any scored run.


Material influence categories (RW-D50): reviewers may count retained knowledge as materially influencing a specific output claim when it (1) supports the claim with relevant evidence or reasoning; (2) qualifies the claim with a material limitation, uncertainty, or condition; or (3) corrects or prevents a misleading conclusion by distinguishing related findings or interpretations. Mere topical similarity, a passing mention, or an irrelevant citation does not count.

Claim-use gate minimum (RW-D51): all 10 treatment outputs are assessed independently. Record every qualifying claim and its retained-knowledge reference; report how many outputs contain at least one independently verified qualifying claim; and report unsupported attribution attempts separately. At least one verified qualifying claim across the treatment condition is required to demonstrate that claim-level attribution works. This minimum does not demonstrate consistent use across all runs. Show the full distribution and do not impose an arbitrary percentage threshold in advance.

Many-to-many claim/knowledge attribution (RW-D52): one accepted KnowledgeItem version may materially influence multiple output claims, and multiple accepted KnowledgeItem versions may materially influence one output claim. Record and independently assess each claimed relationship; do not impose a one-to-one mapping.

Ambiguous claim/knowledge attribution (RW-D53): reviewers classify each claimed relationship as (1) qualifying, when it meets a D50 material-influence category; (2) non-qualifying, when documented but not materially influential; or (3) indeterminate, when available evidence is insufficient to decide. Only qualifying relationships count toward the acceptance gate. Preserve indeterminate relationships in reporting rather than silently treating them as successes or failures.

Reviewer disagreement for claim-use attribution (RW-D54): two reviewers independently classify each claimed claim-to-knowledge relationship. Agreement establishes the provisional classification. Disagreement triggers adjudication by a third qualified reviewer; the adjudicated result becomes final, while both original judgments and the disagreement remain recorded.

Claim-use review evidence record (RW-D55): retain, for every reviewed relationship, the output ID and claim ID; exact KnowledgeItem ID and version; originating LearningChange ID; reviewer classification; a concise explanation identifying the specific evidence or reasoning supporting that classification; reviewer identity; and adjudication status where applicable. This explanation records the reviewer’s evidence-based judgment, not a reconstruction of hidden model reasoning.

Claim-use reporting levels (RW-D56): report (1) relationship-level counts for qualifying, non-qualifying, and indeterminate relationships, including adjudicated disagreements; (2) output-level coverage, showing how many of the 10 treatment outputs contain at least one qualifying relationship; and (3) condition-level aggregate results across all 10 treatment outputs, with the full distribution visible. This prevents numerous references concentrated in one output from masking the absence of qualifying use in other outputs.

Claim identification for attribution review (RW-D57): reviewers identify claims from the actual output, not solely from model-supplied claim IDs. Assign each distinct factual or interpretive claim a stable ID within its output. Split compound sentences when their components can be independently evaluated; repeated statements of the same underlying claim are not separate claims. Keep the original output immutable; claim IDs and annotations belong to the review record.

Claim review scope (RW-D58): identify claims by meaning rather than sentence count. Include factual and interpretive assertions about the subject; include headlines and section headings when they make substantive assertions; and include uncertainty statements that assert evidence is limited, conflicting, or insufficient. Exclude purely rhetorical transitions, questions, and stylistic language unless they imply a factual or interpretive assertion.

Independent claim identification and reconciliation (RW-D59): both reviewers independently identify and annotate claims from each immutable output before discussing the output or one another's inventories. They then reconcile differences, including disputed claim boundaries, omitted claims, and possible duplicate identification. Refer unresolved disagreements to a third qualified reviewer. Preserve both original inventories and the reconciled inventory; reconciliation must not overwrite the independent records.

Claim attribution completeness (RW-D60): retain every identified claim in the reconciled inventory regardless of whether any retained-knowledge relationship is attributed to it. Record claimed claim-to-knowledge relationships separately; do not presume that every claim should use retained knowledge. Report mutually exclusive claim counts in three groups: claims with at least one qualifying relationship; claims with one or more attribution attempts but no qualifying relationship (including non-qualifying and/or indeterminate relationships only); and claims with no attribution attempt. An unattributed claim is not a failure: the experiment tests whether retained knowledge is used where relevant, not whether every output claim cites memory. This preserves the complete claim inventory as the reporting denominator without pressuring the model to attach memory references to every claim.

Attribution reference eligibility (RW-D61): a claim-to-knowledge relationship may qualify only when it identifies the exact KnowledgeItem ID and version through an application-validated immutable TaskKnowledgeReference, and the Task's Context manifest proves that the same version entered that Task's Context. The referenced version must have been eligible for the relevant Team when that Context was constructed. Eligibility is evaluated at that historical point: later retirement or replacement does not retroactively invalidate a reference that was valid at Context construction. A nonexistent item/version, cross-Team reference, wrong version, version that was ineligible at Context construction, or version absent from the Task's Context is an unsupported attribution attempt and can never qualify. Preserve the validation failure reason in the review evidence and unsupported-attempt reporting. Do not silently substitute a different or newer KnowledgeItem version.

Technical validation before substantive attribution review (RW-D62): every claimed relationship that fails the deterministic checks in RW-D61 remains visible as an attribution attempt and is classified non-qualifying, with its specific validation failure reason preserved. Do not send a technically invalid relationship through subjective material-influence review; it cannot qualify regardless of the explanation supplied. Only relationships that pass deterministic reference validation proceed to independent reviewer assessment of whether the retained knowledge materially influenced the claim under D50 and the qualifying/non-qualifying/indeterminate classifications in D53. Report technical validation failures separately from reviewer judgments that a technically valid reference was not materially influential. This makes technical integrity failure distinguishable from a valid reference that does not substantively support the claim.

Definition of a claim-level attribution attempt (RW-D63): an attribution attempt exists only when the output or its accompanying structured attribution data explicitly associates an identified claim with one or more specific KnowledgeItem references. A general statement that retained knowledge or memory was used does not establish claim-level attribution. A specific reference counts as an attempt even when it fails D61's deterministic validation and is therefore classified non-qualifying under D62. Reviewers must not invent missing KnowledgeItem references or infer use solely because relevant knowledge was available to the Task. If no explicit claim-level reference is present, the claim remains unattributed; it is not counted as a failed attribution attempt. This keeps attribution reporting grounded in recorded references rather than model assertions or reviewer reconstruction.

Claim-level roll-up across multiple attribution relationships (RW-D64): assign every identified claim exactly one mutually exclusive reporting category. A claim with at least one qualifying relationship belongs to the qualifying category even when it also has invalid, non-qualifying, or indeterminate relationships. A claim with no qualifying relationship but at least one explicit attribution attempt belongs to the attempted-but-not-qualifying category. A claim with no explicit reference belongs to the unattributed category. Preserve relationship-level reporting for every claimed reference: the claim-level category must not hide additional failed or indeterminate relationships attached to that claim. Technical validation failures remain included in the non-qualifying relationship total and must also be reported separately. This allows claim-level coverage to remain mutually exclusive without discarding relationship-level integrity information.

Claim-use coverage reporting (RW-D65): for each of the 10 treatment outputs, report both raw counts and percentages for the three mutually exclusive claim categories defined in D64: claims with at least one qualifying relationship; claims with explicit attribution attempts but no qualifying relationship; and claims with no attribution attempt. Also report aggregate counts and percentages across all treatment claims. Show the per-output distribution alongside the pooled treatment result; a pooled percentage alone could conceal inconsistent use across outputs. Calculate percentages using each output's reconciled claim inventory as its denominator, and use the combined reconciled claim inventory for the aggregate denominator. Do not add a new percentage acceptance threshold. The predeclared D51 gate remains at least one independently verified qualifying claim across the treatment condition; the full distribution supports interpretation of consistency without changing that gate.

Claim-level retained-knowledge use (RW-D49): count use only when a specific output claim is linked to the exact accepted KnowledgeItem version and originating LearningChange; an immutable TaskKnowledgeReference and Context manifest establish that the same version entered that Task's Context; application validation confirms identity, version, eligibility, and Context linkage; and independent reviewers judge whether the knowledge materially supports, qualifies, or informs the claim. Retrieval or Context availability alone is availability, not use, and an unsupported model assertion of use is insufficient. This provides auditable claim-level use without claiming access to hidden reasoning or proving causation.

Source citation and claim attribution (RW-D48): require explicit packet-ID attribution for direct factual claims. For interpretations and implications, identify the packet evidence on which they rely and distinguish the analysis from source-reported findings. Explicitly acknowledge uncertainty and material points the packet does not establish; do not present unsupported claims as established facts. This enables claim-level review without imposing identical wording or a rigid citation style.

Scored output length and presentation (RW-D47): apply one common word limit to all 20 outputs, choosing its exact value after the story and brief are drafted. Require material to appear within the defined sections, identify sources consistently by packet IDs, and prohibit extra material outside the required sections. Do not require identical wording or conclusions, and do not reward verbosity; concise outputs receive full credit when they adequately cover the required elements.

Scored output structure (RW-D46): require a consistent structured editorial analysis with a headline, central finding, why it matters, evidence and source comparison, limitations and uncertainty, and what remains unknown. This supports comparable review without prescribing conclusions or wording. Set the exact common word limit when drafting the brief and apply it equally to all runs.

Scored assignment brief structure (RW-D45): use the same fixed structure for every run: task, intended audience, scope and exclusions, evidence rule, required analytical elements, and consistent output format and length limit. The evidence rule limits the model to the supplied source packet and requires it to distinguish source-supported findings from interpretation and uncertainty. Required elements include the central finding, significance, limitations, uncertainty, and comparison of relevant sources. The brief must not disclose the answer key, preferred conclusions, or specific expected claims. Control and treatment receive identical instructions.

Source packet and assignment freeze order (RW-D44): select a candidate story, collect and review candidate primary sources, then freeze the source packet and manifest. Write the scored newsroom assignment brief using only that frozen packet. Prepare the independent answer key, then freeze the brief, answer key, and rubric before scored runs. This order prevents the assignment brief from steering source selection toward a desired conclusion.

Newsroom story selection (RW-D43): choose a story with accessible primary research or equivalent evidence; important factual claims must be directly verifiable against the frozen source packet; the subject must offer meaningful significance, limitations, or uncertainty to analyze; scope must remain bounded; and sources must be preservable for reproducibility. Avoid breaking news and rapidly changing subjects so the experiment measures retained experience rather than changes in available information.

Source-packet size (RW-D42): determine the number of sources by evidentiary sufficiency rather than a predetermined numerical target. The packet must contain enough relevant primary evidence to establish the central finding and its material qualifications, limitations, and conflicting evidence where that evidence affects interpretation. Keep it bounded enough for reviewers to verify claims reliably. Do not add sources merely to make the packet appear comprehensive.

Source corpus selection and freeze (RW-D41): prefer primary sources such as original research papers, official datasets, and direct institutional publications wherever available. Every included source must contribute relevant evidence. Include sources needed to represent important qualifications, limitations, and conflicting findings rather than selecting only evidence for one narrative. Preserve exact source files or stable archived versions with retrieval dates and cryptographic hashes. After the corpus is frozen, neither control nor treatment runs may receive additional sources. Record important candidate sources that were excluded and the reasons for exclusion. Freeze the corpus and version manifest before scored runs.

The exact newsroom beat, source corpus, task brief, and independent outcome set are not selected here. They must be frozen before the first scored harness run.

The Operator has confirmed the target product claims (RW-D07), the inclusion boundary for KnowledgeUseAssessment (RW-D08), the measurement boundary for retrieval evaluation (RW-D09), the controlled comparison methodology for downstream benefit (RW-D10), the exclusion of persistent Artifacts/publishing as a foundation dependency (RW-D11), the boundary between operational Consultation and broader human input curation/domain guidance (RW-D12), and the timing decision to defer in-depth design of the general input stream until after R8 testing (RW-D13), and the decision to use a human-configured Team while deferring self-service setup (RW-D14), and the decision to retain existing Team/generalist defaults without expanding domain-pack machinery (RW-D15). The canonical Thread-creation path is also fixed: `triage_signal` alone creates Threads through `new_thread`; `update_thread` and `operator_update` refine existing Threads, with same-Team fixtures used for their commit-path proofs (RW-D16). The delivery order is also fixed: establish the deterministic human `operator_update` path in RW-2 before adding model-driven `update_thread` in RW-3, with identical proposal/commit validation for both (RW-D17). Preserve the established mixed proposal-batch contract: accepted proposals commit atomically as a set, invalid proposals produce rejection Events, and the Task fails only when every proposal fails (RW-D18). A rejected model-generated `update_thread` proposal is terminal for that attempt: record its rejection Event, with no automatic model repair/re-proposal; a later separately initiated Task may try again (RW-D19). Model-call verification is split between deterministic stub-provider automated tests and a separate live provider smoke test; ordinary CI has no network/provider dependency and does not rely on nondeterministic model output (RW-D20). In the first RW release, Hypotheses and their supporting/challenging evidence use the shared EvidenceLink model, while existing Evaluation and distillation behavior remains unchanged; automatic linkage or new distillation behavior is deferred unless the frozen newsroom scenario demonstrates a concrete requirement (RW-D21). The first newsroom value-harness beat is science and technology reporting; the corpus, brief, outcome checks, rubric, run count, and thresholds remain open until explicitly frozen before scored runs (RW-D22). The first scored experiment uses a fixed, curated, versioned corpus of primary sources rather than unrestricted live web search, so source-discovery variability does not confound the initial evaluation (RW-D23). Its task is to produce an evidence-grounded science and technology news analysis from that packet: identify the central finding, explain significance, distinguish established results from uncertainty, compare sources, and trace claims to evidence. The brief may be refined during test preparation, but the scored version and evaluation criteria must be frozen before scored runs (RW-D24). Evaluation has two separate components: structured claim verification against a pre-prepared source-grounded answer key, and blinded independent editorial assessment of interpretation, significance, uncertainty handling, and synthesis. The answer key and reviewer rubric must be frozen before scored runs (RW-D25). The rubric totals 100 points, split 50/50 between claim verification and editorial assessment; critical factual errors are reported separately and cannot be hidden by the aggregate score (RW-D26). The initial experiment uses 10 control and 10 treatment runs with identical source packet and task brief, treatment-only retained experience through normal retrieval/Context, blinded review, all-run reporting, and run-state isolation to prevent cross-run or cross-condition contamination. Results are an initial bounded engineering experiment, not broad statistical generality (RW-D27). Predeclared acceptance gates: treatment mean total score at least 10 points above control; no increase in critical factual errors, with all such errors disclosed and examined; and demonstrated claim-level use of retrieved knowledge. Report distributions, uncertainty, and individual runs; do not claim statistical proof (RW-D28). Scored runs start from one frozen baseline; treatment alone receives the same frozen retained-experience snapshot; scored-run writes cannot be retrieved by other scored runs; retrieval and Context processing remain identical apart from snapshot availability. This isolates experience availability from in-experiment learning (RW-D29). Application-written, claim-level TaskKnowledgeReference and the minimal typed KnowledgeUseAssessment are therefore part of the proposed RW foundation scope. The assessment must validate exact knowledge-version and LearningChange identity, the later Task's terminal Outcome, and immutable Context evidence. It is an auditable use assessment, not a knowledge mutation, approval, decision, or causal finding. Any broader autonomous evaluation or learning loop remains deferred. Retrieval-quality evaluation, label provenance, small-sample reporting, and the blinded control/treatment comparison are harness methodology rather than production subsystems. Persistent publishing/delivery is not part of the first newsroom foundation claim. Retrieval quality, exposure, claim-level attribution, and downstream benefit remain distinct measurements.

## 2. Accepted architecture constraints

These are existing accepted contracts, not new decisions made by this record.

### 2.1 EvidenceLink is one shared evidence model

- [ADR-0007](../decisions/architecture%20decision%20records%20%28ADR-0001%20-%20ADR-0019%29.md#adr-0007-generalized-evidence-links-with-deterministic-validation) defines polymorphic EvidenceLink subjects **hypothesis | evaluation** and sources **signal | outcome | artifact**. Quotes are validated deterministically against immutable hashed Snapshots; only **supports** and **challenges** can anchor ConfidenceChanges.
- [ADR-0020](../decisions/ADR-0020-consultations-waiting-happens-between-tasks.md) adds **consultation** as an EvidenceLink source. A Consultation answer is an immutable hashed Snapshot and can be cited using the same quote-validation rule.
- [ADR-0021](../decisions/ADR-0021-operator-update-path-human-as-first-class-worker.md) requires **operator_update** to use the same proposal and commit validation as **update_thread**. Human authorship is provenance, not a validation exemption.

RW must implement these accepted contracts through the existing EvidenceLink concept. A sibling evidence table or a human-only bypass is not the default design.

### 2.2 Current implementation is narrower than the accepted contract

At the code baseline audited for this checkpoint (**29c954fe8685e0a5ddb9b205277a28b1a158c57e**):

- **core/models.py** has an Evaluation-only EvidenceLink subject.
- Its supported source kinds are **signal** and **outcome**.
- It does not implement Hypothesis subjects, Artifact sources, or Consultation sources.
- **Thread**, **Hypothesis**, **ConfidenceChange**, and **SignalDisposition** are not ORM models in this baseline.
- The task names **triage_signal**, **update_thread**, **operator_update**, and **apply_decay** appear in specifications and an ingestion Event-scope allow-list; that allow-list is not an implementation of those Tasks.

This is an implementation gap against the accepted architecture, not a reason to replace the accepted model. Recheck the audit after B6 and immediately before RW-1.

### 2.3 Evidence-model implementation rule

The implementation plan should extend the existing **evidence_links** contract to the accepted subject/source vocabulary while preserving current Evaluation evidence, quote/hash validation, same-Team ownership, uniqueness, and rejection semantics.

The exact relational representation, foreign-key strategy, and migration sequence remain RW-1 engineering work. If the accepted polymorphic contract cannot be implemented without changing an accepted decision, stop and submit an ADR addendum for Operator review before coding. Do not silently narrow ADR-0007 or create a parallel evidence system.

Artifact source support may be staged until an Artifact entity exists, but the accepted target vocabulary must remain explicit. Consultation support is not optional in the accepted target: ADR-0020 already includes it. The order in which those source types become operational must be stated in the RW-1 migration plan.

## 4. Proposed RW delivery shape

The current planning proposal is:

1. **RW-1 — persistence:** add the durable world-memory records and reconcile the existing EvidenceLink table with accepted ADRs.
2. **RW-2 — deterministic commit:** implement the human operator_update path through the ordinary Task, proposal, validation, and commit gates.
3. **RW-3 — model update:** implement update_thread with bounded Context, typed proposals, existing generation/cost controls, and rejection Events.
4. **RW-4 — triage and read path:** add the smallest deterministic triage needed by the newsroom scenario and expose accepted active Hypotheses to bounded Task Context.
5. **RW-5 — decay:** implement the deterministic apply_decay contract without allowing decay to reject Hypotheses or refresh last_touched_at.

This is a proposed sequence for RW-0 to freeze. It is not implementation authorization.
